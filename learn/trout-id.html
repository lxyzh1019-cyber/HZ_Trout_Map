Catch limits in Alberta can differ by species. Know which trout you have before you keep it. Look at the spots, the pattern on the back and the lower fins.

Rainbow trout

Many small black spots on the back, the sides and the tail. A pink or red band along each side.

Brook trout

Pale, worm-like lines on a dark back. Red spots with blue rings on the sides. A white front edge on the lower fins.

Brown trout

Golden-brown body. Black spots and red or orange spots, often with pale rings. Few or no spots on the tail.

Tiger trout

A maze of wavy lines over the whole body, like a tiger's stripes. No clear round spots. It is a cross between a brown trout and a brook trout.

Cutthroat trout

A red or orange slash under each side of the lower jaw. Black spots on the sides, more of them toward the tail.

Westslope cutthroat trout

A cutthroat with small black spots, mostly toward the tail and above the side line. Few spots on the front, lower part of the body. A red slash under the jaw.

When you are not sure

Cutthroat and rainbow trout can breed together. Their young can have marks of both. If you cannot tell, treat the fish as the species with the stricter limit, or release it.
