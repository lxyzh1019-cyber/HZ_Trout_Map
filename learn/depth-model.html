This guide shows how to read a lake in 3D. The model below is a real surveyed lake. Turn it with a finger, zoom with two fingers, and tap any spot to read the depth. Each section has a Show on the model button: it turns on only that layer and sets an example wind or ice. The spots and layers are estimates from the lakebed shape, the forecast and the sun. Use them as a place to start, not as a promise.

Legal is not safe. Ice thickness is never forecast. Check the ice yourself before you walk out.

Sample lake: Lower Kananaskis Lake, Alberta Geological Survey lakebed survey. The example wind is from the west at 20 km/h, unless a section says other.

Fish depth today

Light blue dots mark the depth where trout likely feed on the day of the Time slider. It is the same range as the tip in the lake panel. The rule, first match wins:

  • Under ice: 1.5–4 m, the under-ice band.
  • Water under 10 °C (after ice-off, late fall): 1–4 m, near shore and on flats.
  • Water 10–18 °C: 2–6 m, along the weed edge and drop-offs.
  • Water over 18 °C on a lake that layers: in or just above the summer layer.
  • Water over 18 °C on a lake that does not layer: from half the lake depth to the bottom; shallow at dawn and dusk.
  • Pressure: steady uses the whole range; rising uses the deeper half; falling uses the shallower half.
  • The range never goes deeper than the lake.

Depth and height

Depth is shown from the water level of the Alberta survey. The key says "Depth at the survey water level; the real level changes." A spot can be a little deeper or shallower today.

The lakebed is drawn taller than it is, so you can see its shape. The Height slider starts at a lake-by-lake value so that each lake looks clear. Default view puts the model back to its first position and zoom.

Wind arrows

The small red arrows show where the wind blows to. The wind sign in the corner shows north, the wind and the sun. Wind pushes surface food and warm water to the shore it blows toward. Fish often feed there.

Wind time

The 3D view starts at the current hour. A time slider and a Play button move through the forecast hours. The arrows, the spots and the fish depth follow the hour.

Wind under 8 km/h

Wind too light to move food. Spots use the drop-offs only.

With less than 8 km/h of wind, the wind moves too little food to matter, so the shore spots come from the drop-offs only.

Possible spots

The orange pins and line are spots you can reach. They match how you fish. Tap a pin for its reason and a Directions link to that spot.

Shore spot: a shore cell where water 3 m or deeper is within a 30 m cast. With wind 8 km/h or more, the shore must face the wind's push, and a longer stretch of open water behind it scores higher. Up to 3, at least 150 m apart.

Trolling route: the 5 m depth line on the downwind half of the lake; with light wind, the whole 5 m line. Faded when no boat launch is listed: then use a car-top boat, canoe or float tube. A shallow lake uses a shallower line.

Ice spot: 1.5–4 m water within about 60 m of a drop-off, scored by the drop size, at least 150 m from the aerator. Up to 3. The text says to check the ice thickness first.

On big lakes the lakebed grid is coarse (one cell can be up to about 130 m), so "within a cast" means the nearest cell; the tap text then says the real distance. The app does not know the shore trails or where you can park. Make sure you can reach a shore spot legally.

Under ice

Under ice: ice spots only, in the 1.5–4 m band next to a drop. Check the ice thickness first.

Under ice there are no wind arrows. The view uses the same ice state that Lake info shows for that day.

Under-ice band

White dots show all 1.5–4 m water. In winter trout feed there, along weed edges and drop-offs, where there is more food and oxygen. It shows where to drill. Tick the layer any time of year, to plan before the ice. The ice spots are the best places in this band.

Structure

Small markers show the shape of the lakebed. Point at a marker on a PC, or tap it on a phone, to see its name and why it matters.

  • Island: land in the lake. Fish its shallow edges and the drop beside it.
  • Point: land that reaches into the lake. Wind and fish both swing around its tip.
  • Hump: the lakebed rises with deeper water around it. Fish its top edge.
  • Basin: a deep hole. Trout hold near basins in summer heat and in mid-winter.
  • Drop-off: the steepest slope of the lake. A good edge to cast along.
  • Flat: a wide shallow shelf, 1–3 m. Weeds and bugs grow here; best at dawn, dusk and in spring.

Drop-offs

Orange dots on the lakebed mark its steepest parts: the steepest 6 % of this lake, and never less than 1 m of drop in 40 m. Trout follow these edges between shallow food and deep safety.

Weed line (estimate)

The green line is the 4 m depth line. In most Alberta lakes weeds stop near this depth, where light no longer reaches the bottom. Trout cruise this edge for bugs and minnows. Clear lakes can grow weeds deeper; brown water stops them sooner.

Summer layer

In July and August a deep lake forms layers. Below the summer layer the water is cold and low in oxygen. The two see-through planes show the layer the app expects for this lake, from June to September and never under ice. Fish in it or just above it. A lake too shallow to layer has no planes.

Deep within a cast

Purple dots mark shore where water 4 m or deeper is within 30 m. These are good bank spots on a hot day or at midday.

Calm shore

Green dots mark the shore the wind blows away from. The water there is flat. It is the best place for a float tube or a fly line on a windy day.

Sun and shade shore

For the hour on the Time slider: dark dots mark the shade shore, where the bank on the sun's side shades the water. Yellow dots mark the sunny shore across from it. On bright days trout come shallower in shade. In spring and fall the sunny shore warms first.

Reported spots

Purple pins are spots reported by anglers, each with its source and date. They come from the Hot spots list of this app. The list is short today, so most lakes have none yet.

Aerator warning

On an aerated lake the aerator keeps water open and ice thin from October to April. The open water moves, and the thin ice around it does not look different. Stay off that area. The app does not know where the aerator is; the signs at the lake show it. The sample lake is not aerated.

Your position

The view asks for your position one time when it opens. If you say no, there is no pin and no second question. The sample model here does not ask.

Only in the 3D view

Boating rules

The trolling route gives the rule for each lake from two sources: the Transport Canada list of boating limits (the law), and the MyWildAlberta lake page. When they differ, the app uses the Transport Canada list and names both. The same rule is in the Rules section of the lake panel. On every Alberta lake the speed limit is 10 km/h within 30 m of shore (Vessel Operation Restriction Regulations, section 2(7); section 2(8) lists the exceptions, such as towing a skier straight out from shore).

When neither source gives a rule, the app says so. Check the signs at the launch.

Credit line

The 3D view shows the Alberta credit line. The Alberta depth chart link stays next to the 3D button in Lake info. Lakes without a lakebed survey keep the depth chart text they have.
