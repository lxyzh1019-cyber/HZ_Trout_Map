The 3D depth model shows the lakebed of 43 stocked lakes that Alberta has surveyed. Turn it with a finger, zoom with two fingers, and tap any spot to read the depth. The spots and layers are estimates from the lakebed shape, the forecast and the sun. Use them as a place to start, not as a promise.

Legal is not safe. Ice thickness is never forecast. Check the ice yourself before you walk out.

Depth and height

Depth is measured from the water level of the Alberta survey. The real level changes from year to year, so a spot can be a little deeper or shallower today.

The lakebed is drawn taller than it is, so you can see its shape. The Height slider changes how much. Default view puts the model back to its first position and zoom.

Wind arrows

The small red arrows show where the wind blows to, for the hour on the Time slider. The wind sign in the corner shows north, the wind and the sun. Play steps through the forecast hours.

Wind pushes surface food and warm water to the shore it blows toward. Fish often feed there. With less than 8 km/h of wind, the wind moves too little food to matter, and the spots use the drop-offs only.

Possible spots

The orange pins and line are spots you can reach. They match how you fish:

  • Shore spot. A shore where water 3 m or deeper is within a 30 m cast. With wind, the shore must face the wind's push. A longer stretch of open water behind it scores higher, because the wind carries more food. Up to 3 spots, at least 150 m apart.
  • Trolling route. The 5 m depth line (less on a shallow lake), on the downwind half of the lake. With light wind it goes around the whole lake. It says if a boat launch is listed, and gives the boating rules. With no launch listed, the line is faded: use a car-top boat, canoe or float tube.
  • Ice spot. Under ice: water 1.5–4 m deep within about 60 m of a drop-off. A bigger drop scores higher. Up to 3 spots, at least 150 m apart. Check the ice thickness first.

The app does not know the shore trails or where you can park. Make sure you can reach a shore spot legally.

Structure

Small markers show the shape of the lakebed. Point at a marker on a PC, or tap it on a phone, to see its name and why it matters.

  • Island: land in the lake. Fish its shallow edges and the drop beside it.
  • Point: land that reaches into the lake. Wind and fish both swing around its tip.
  • Hump: the lakebed rises with deeper water around it. Fish its top edge.
  • Basin: a deep hole. Trout hold near basins in summer heat and in mid-winter.
  • Drop-off: the steepest slope of the lake. A good edge to cast along.
  • Flat: a wide shallow shelf, 1–3 m. Weeds and bugs grow here; best at dawn, dusk and in spring.

Drop-offs

Orange dots on the lakebed mark its steepest parts: the steepest 6 % of this lake, and never less than 1 m of drop in 40 m. Trout follow these edges between shallow food and deep safety.

Weed line (estimate)

The green line is the 4 m depth line. In most Alberta lakes weeds stop near this depth, where light no longer reaches the bottom. Trout cruise this edge for bugs and minnows. Clear lakes can grow weeds deeper; brown water stops them sooner.

Summer layer

In July and August a deep lake forms layers. Below the summer layer the water is cold and low in oxygen. The two see-through planes show the layer the app expects for this lake. Fish in it or just above it. A lake too shallow to layer has no planes.

Deep within a cast

Purple dots mark shore where water 4 m or deeper is within 30 m. These are good bank spots on a hot day or at midday.

Calm shore

Green dots mark the shore the wind blows away from. The water there is flat. It is the best place for a float tube or a fly line on a windy day.

Sun and shade shore

For the hour on the Time slider: dark dots mark the shade shore, where the bank on the sun's side shades the water. Yellow dots mark the sunny shore across from it. On bright days trout come shallower in shade. In spring and fall the sunny shore warms first.

Reported spots

Purple pins are spots reported by anglers, each with its source and date. They come from the Hot spots list of this app. The list is short today, so most lakes have none yet.

Under-ice band

White dots mark all water 1.5–4 m deep. In winter trout feed in this band, along weed edges and drop-offs, where there is more food and oxygen than in the deep basin. It shows where to drill. The ice spots are the best places in this band.

Aerator warning

On an aerated lake the aerator keeps water open and ice thin from October to April. The open water moves, and the thin ice around it does not look different. Stay off that area. The app does not know where the aerator is; the signs at the lake show it.

Boating rules

The trolling route gives the rule for each lake from two sources: the Transport Canada list of boating limits (the law), and the MyWildAlberta lake page. When they differ, the app uses the Transport Canada list and names both. On every Alberta lake the speed limit is 10 km/h within 30 m of shore.

When neither source gives a rule, the app says so. Check the signs at the launch.
