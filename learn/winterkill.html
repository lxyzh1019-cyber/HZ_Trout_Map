Winterkill is when fish die under the ice because the water runs out of oxygen. It happens most in shallow lakes and ponds. Many stocked lakes in Alberta are shallow.

Why a shallow lake loses oxygen

Ice seals the lake from the air. No new oxygen comes in from the wind and waves.

Snow on the ice blocks the light. Water plants stop making oxygen when they get no light.

Dead plants and algae rot on the bottom. Rotting uses up oxygen all winter.

A shallow lake holds less water, so it holds less oxygen at freeze-up. It runs out sooner. A long winter with deep snow is the worst case.

What aerators do

An aerator pumps air into the water or moves the water at the surface. This adds oxygen and keeps a patch of the lake open. Fish can survive the winter in the water near it.

Alberta and the Alberta Conservation Association aerate some stocked lakes. The "Aerated" filter on the map shows lakes that they name as aerated.

An aerated lake can still have a winterkill in a hard winter. Aeration lowers the risk. It does not remove it.

Stay away from the open water

Near an aerator there is open water, and the ice around it is thin and weak. The edge of the thin ice can be far from the open water.

  • Stay behind the fences and signs.
  • Keep children and dogs away from the fenced area.
  • Do not drive or walk near the aerator, even if the ice looks solid.
