Ice changes from place to place

Ice can be thick in one spot and thin a few steps away. It is often thin in these places:

  • near aerators and their open water;
  • near inlets, outlets and springs, where water moves;
  • on rivers and streams, where the current wears the ice from below;
  • near pressure ridges and cracks;
  • near docks, rocks, reeds and fallen trees.

White or grey ice, and ice with snow on it, is weaker than clear ice. Ice also gets weaker on warm days and in spring.

Before you go on the ice

  • Check the ice thickness yourself, often, as you go.
  • Never go onto the ice alone.
  • Tell someone where you are going and when you will be back.
  • Wear a lifejacket or a floating suit, and carry ice picks.
  • Stay off river ice.
