Each lake gets two scores for each hour. The Bite score is about the fish. The Day out score is about you.

Each lake gets the forecast for its own spot. The forecast comes in cells a quarter of a degree wide. Two lakes near each other can be in different cells, so their scores can differ.

The Bite score

The Bite score adds up five things. Each one has a weight out of 100:

  • Light: 40. Dawn and dusk score best. Cloud lifts a bright afternoon.
  • Water temperature: 35. Each species has a range it likes. The score uses the best match among the fish in the lake.
  • Pressure trend: 15. The direction of change matters, not the number. See the barometer guide.
  • Wind: 6. A breeze that puts a ripple on the water scores best. Flat calm scores lower, and strong wind lowest.
  • Rain: 4. Dry hours score best. Light rain lowers the score a little, and heavy rain more.

The moon is applied last, as a small multiplier. It moves the score by 5% at most, up or down.

Gusts are not part of the Bite score. When gusts reach 40 km/h, a warning shows instead.

Pressure counts for less on days further ahead. A pressure forecast for next week is much less sure than one for tomorrow.

The Day out score

The Day out score tells you how pleasant and safe the day is on the water. It uses three things.

  • Wind: the wind speed plus half the gap between the wind and the gusts.
  • Rain: how much rain, and the chance of rain.
  • Air temperature: below 5 °C or above 28 °C lowers the score.

Wind counts more on a big lake, because waves build over open water. Lake size scales the wind:

  • Small lake, under 10 ha: wind × 0.7.
  • Medium lake: wind × 1.0.
  • Large lake, over 200 ha: wind × 1.3.

Some lakes have no published size. Those use the medium scale, and the app says "size unknown".

The bands

  • Prime: 75 and up.
  • Good: 60 and up.
  • Fair: 42 and up.
  • Poor: below 42.

A score is a guide, not a promise. Fish do not read forecasts.
